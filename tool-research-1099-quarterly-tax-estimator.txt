3) EVIDENCE SUMMARY (WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWS)
==================================================
A) Official rules create recurring, unavoidable demand
- IRS estimated tax FAQ confirms individuals generally must pay estimated tax if they expect to owe >= $1,000 after withholding and credits.
- IRS explains penalty-avoidance thresholds (safe harbor logic): generally pay at least 90% of current-year tax, or 100% of prior-year tax (110% if prior-year AGI > $150,000).
- IRS/TAS materials outline quarterly due dates for the 2026 tax year:
  - Q1: April 15, 2026
  - Q2: June 15, 2026
  - Q3: September 15, 2026
  - Q4: January 15, 2027
- IRS guidance confirms underpayment penalties are real and references Form 2210 logic.

Why this matters for product:
- A deadline-driven tool naturally gets repeat visits (at least 4 times per year per user).
- Users need confidence, not just a rough number.

B) The addressable audience is meaningful
- BLS Employment Situation (January 2026) reports ~8.6 million unincorporated self-employed workers.
- US Census Nonemployer Statistics (latest release for 2022) reports ~29.8 million nonemployer establishments.

Why this matters for product:
- Even a small share of this audience can support large organic traffic and strong conversion volume.

C) Income reporting complexity is increasing for independent workers
- IRS gig economy and 1099-K pages emphasize that gig/freelance income is taxable and must be reported even when forms are missing or thresholds are not met.

Why this matters for product:
- Users are confused by mixed 1099-NEC/1099-K/W-2 income streams.
- A calculator that handles mixed-income scenarios solves real confusion.

D) Competitor landscape exists, but many tools are shallow or lead-gen-first
Observed patterns from competitor pages:
- Some calculators are embedded inside paid tax software or account-based flows.
- Some free tools use very limited input models (simple YTD fields only).
- Many tools do not clearly explain formula assumptions, safe harbor branch logic, or uneven-income methods.

Why this matters for product:
- A transparent, explainable, no-login estimator with clear assumptions is a defensible differentiation.

4) COMPETITOR GAP ANALYSIS
==================================================
Competitor categories reviewed:
- Tax software ecosystems (TurboTax, TaxAct, TaxSlayer Pro context pages)
- Accounting-firm lead-gen calculators (example: quarterly calculator pages with consultation CTA)
- Small independent calculators/blog tools

Common gaps found:
- No clear "calculation trace" (users cannot see how final number was produced).
- Weak treatment of uneven income (many freelancers earn lumpy income by quarter).
- Limited handling of mixed-income scenarios (W-2 + 1099 + spouse income + withholding).
- Poor scenario planning ("what if income rises 20% in Q3?").
- Weak export/reminder utility (users still need a payment plan workflow).

Product gap to own:
- "Explainable quarterly tax planning" with:
  - Safe harbor mode vs current-year mode
  - Equal installment vs annualized installment view
  - Quarter-by-quarter catch-up plan
  - Assumption transparency and downloadable summary

11) RISKS + MITIGATIONS
==================================================
Risk: Tax law/rate changes can invalidate logic.
Mitigation: Versioned tax-year engine + visible "Tax Year" selector + annual QA checklist.

Risk: Users may misinterpret estimates as legal advice.
Mitigation: Clear disclaimer + "educational estimate" language + links to IRS sources.

Risk: State tax complexity is large.
Mitigation: Launch federal-first; then add highest-traffic states in phases.

Risk: Trust barrier for financial calculations.
Mitigation: Transparent formulas, explicit assumptions, and source links.
