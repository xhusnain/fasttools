## 3. The Mathematics of Time: Algorithms of Affection

### 3.1 The Gregorian Complexity and the Leap Year Problem

At the core of any robust anniversary calculator is the handling of the Gregorian calendar's irregularities. A standard year is 365 days, but the astronomical year is approximately 365.2425 days. To account for this, the calendar introduces a leap day every four years.

**The Leap Year Problem:**
Consider a couple married on February 29, 2020.
*   **Strict Calculation**: Their first "calendar" anniversary isn't until 2024.
*   **Social Calculation**: Most celebrate on Feb 28th or March 1st.

A high-quality calculator must explicitly handle this logic. Simply subtracting years often leads to "off-by-one" errors in day counts. The [FastTools Anniversary Calculator](/fun-tools/anniversary) utilizes precise date-object manipulation to ensure accurate "Total Days" counts, preventing the drift that plagues simpler tools.

### 4.1 The Korean Phenomenon: The Cult of 100 Days (Baek-il)

While Western culture fixates on the solar year, South Korean relationship culture operates on a faster, decimal-based cadence. The most significant is the **100-Day Anniversary (Baek-il)**.

*   **The 100th Day**: Acts as a "probationary period" graduation. Couples who make it here are seen as moving from "casual" to "committed."

## 5. The Digital Toolkit: Manual Calculation Methods

While our calculator provides instant results, some "Data Enthusiasts" prefer to maintain their own logs.

### 5.1 Excel and The Hidden DATEDIF Function

Microsoft Excel contains a "hidden" function called `DATEDIF`.

*   **Years**: `=DATEDIF(A1, B1, "y")`
*   **Months**: `=DATEDIF(A1, B1, "ym")`
*   **Days**: `=DATEDIF(A1, B1, "md")`

> **Warning**: Microsoft advises caution with the "md" argument as it can have bugs. A safer formula for total days is simply `=B1 - A1`.

### 5.2 Google Sheets and Cloud Tracking

For couples who want a shared tracker, Google Sheets is superior.
*   **Days**: `=DAYS(end_date, start_date)`

**The "Working Days" of Love:**
For office couples, try the `NETWORKDAYS` function to see how many "Monday-to-Fridays" you've weathered together, excluding weekends!
*   **Formula**: `=NETWORKDAYS(start_date, end_date)`
